## The sub-agent paradigm is a dead end

Most current AI frameworks ask you to delegate to agents: an "architect" agent, a "tester" agent, a "reviewer" agent. The more you delegate, the less you control. Context scatters. Decisions become opaque. You lose control of your own project.

**Lytos makes the opposite choice.**

No autonomous agents. No personas deciding for you. The AI stays a tool — powerful, but directed. You define the intent. You validate every step. The model executes. You govern.

---

> A persona doesn't specialize an agent — it just shifts the vocabulary of its approximations.

## Your AI starts from zero every session

Without structure, every conversation starts over. You re-explain the architecture. You repeat the constraints. You correct the same mistakes. The model generates generic code, disconnected from your real project.

The result: hours lost reframing, inconsistencies piling up, and an AI that never truly understands your project.

---

<p style="text-align: center; font-size: 1.2rem; font-weight: 700; margin: 2rem auto; max-width: 700px; color: var(--sl-color-accent);">Agile taught teams to iterate together. Lytos teaches them to iterate with AI.</p>

## Your context belongs to you

With classic cloud tools, your project context lives on their servers, in their formats, dependent on their APIs. Switching models means starting over.

With Lytos, everything is Markdown in your Git repo. Your manifest, your standards, your project memory: version-controlled, portable, readable by any model.

Claude today. GPT or Gemini tomorrow. Your context doesn't move.

---

> Your project's sovereignty is non-negotiable — regardless of which model you choose.

## 5 pillars. One complete method.

Lytos structures your collaboration with AI in five interlocking layers:

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Intent" icon="document">

  </Card>
  <Card title="Design" icon="puzzle">
    Your session playbooks. How to approach a code review, a refactor, a deployment. Reproducible workflows, tailored to your project.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Standards" icon="approve-check">

  </Card>
  <Card title="Memory" icon="star">
    The project's living memory. Architecture, known bugs, emerging patterns, business context. What the AI must remember from one session to the next.

## Operational in 2 minutes

```bash
npm install -g lytos-cli
lyt init
```

`lyt init` generates the complete structure in your project: `manifest.md`, `skills/`, `rules/`, `issue-board/`, `memory/`. Everything in Markdown. Everything in your repo.

---

## Works with your current setup

Lytos is not another tool to learn. It's a layer of structure on top of what you already use.

- **Claude Code** — native adapter included
- **Cursor** — `.cursor/rules` generated automatically
- **OpenAI / GPT** — system-prompt adapter included
- **Gemini, Mistral, and all others** — Markdown is universal

Your tools. Your models. Your method.
